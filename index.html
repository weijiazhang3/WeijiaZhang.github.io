About me

From research questions to real materials.

I recently completed an M.Sc. in Chemical Engineering at McGill University, following a B.A.Sc. in Chemical Engineering at the University of British Columbia.

My work brings together hands-on fabrication, physical characterization, and data analysis. At McGill, I studied ionic and electronic charge transport in M13 bacteriophage films and fabricated the microelectrodes used to characterize them. At UBC, I developed and tested bio-based wood-particle foams.

I am interested in R&D and engineering opportunities involving microfabrication, electronic materials, polymers, biomaterials, and materials characterization.

Selected projects

Research experience

Two experimental projects anchor my research experience: understanding electrical behavior in protein-based materials, and improving the processing and performance of sustainable polymer composites.

McGill University · M.Sc. Research

Charge Transport in Protein Fibers

Dorval Lab · 2024–2026

Investigated how amino-acid identity and substitution position relate to humidity-dependent ionic and electronic transport across 32 engineered M13 bacteriophage mutants.

  • Fabricated Ti/Au interdigitated electrodes on glass using photolithography, e-beam evaporation, and lift-off.
  • Measured I–V responses and current decay under controlled humidity using a semiconductor device analyzer.
  • Characterized samples with AFM, DLS, zeta potential, CD, and SDS-PAGE.
  • Investigated lift-off defects and proposed process improvements to enhance electrode reproducibility.

My primary role was sample preparation, device fabrication, measurement, and analysis; I did not perform the saturation mutagenesis.

University of British Columbia · Undergraduate Research

Sustainable Wood Foams with PVA

Sustainable Nano Biocomposites Lab · 2023

Developed wood-particle foams using polyvinyl alcohol and cellulosic fibers to increase bio-based content while maintaining useful insulation and mechanical performance.

  • Optimized PVA content and molecular weight, fiber loading, and water-to-pulp ratio.
  • Measured foamability, stability, compressive behavior, density, and thermal conductivity.
  • Related performance to pore morphology using microscopy and micro-CT.
  • Achieved full PVA replacement by BCTMP fibers in a tested formulation, with improved compressive strength.
